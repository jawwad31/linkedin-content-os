2Plain-language guide to the wireframes

A wireframe is a rough floor plan of one screen. It shows what is on the screen and where, not colors or fonts. The 40 wireframes in turn 1 below are explained here in words, task by task. You do not need to study them. If this guide makes sense, the design is fine.

How to read the boxes

Gray bars are text. Real wording comes in Phase 2.
Main action
The one main thing to do on that screen. There is only one per screen.
Other action
A secondary choice.
Not available
A dashed button means this person is not allowed. A line of text explains who can allow it.
Single-use Unused
Small labels that state facts, such as whether an entry can be reused.
!Notice
A message you must read before going on.
a1
The screen's name. Use it in chat, for example "change a2".

The app in one minute

Today the app has nine menu items. It now has six.

BeforeOverview, Content Bank, Research, Calendar, Ideas, Posts, Publish, Newsletter, Settings
AfterContent Bank, Calendar, Posts, Newsletter, Research, Settings
  1. Content Bank holds your source material. Ideas are merged into it.
  2. Calendar shows what is scheduled, by date.
  3. Posts holds drafts, reviews and publishing. It opens on what needs you. Overview and Publish are merged into it.
  4. Newsletter holds your newsletter editions.
  5. Research holds your saved prompt and briefings.
  6. Settings holds your business profile and team access.

Each company has its own account. There is no workspace switching and no agency level.

Task 1: turn a saved entry into a draft post 1b

  1. Open Content Bank. Each row is one entry and says whether it can be used once or many times. a1
  2. Press Create post on a row. A form opens with the entry attached and the fields filled in: topic, instructions, reader, takeaway, pillar, post type and funnel stage. Change anything you like. a2
  3. Press Draft post. A progress screen shows each step. It takes 20 to 60 seconds. You can leave, and the draft will be waiting in Posts. a3
  4. The draft appears with quality-check notes. Press Review draft. a4

Task 2: put an entry on the calendar 1c

  1. Open an entry and press Add to Calendar. b1
  2. Choose this month or next month, then click the date you want the post made on. b2
  3. If that date already has a post, you are asked whether to add another. Yes puts two posts on that day. b3
  4. Press Add. The entry is reserved for that date. You can write the post now or later. b4

Replan month on the calendar lets you move several reserved entries to new dates in one go. There is no monthly plan screen. The mix of post types for the month is adjusted automatically in the background.

Task 3: see the month 1d

Open Calendar. Days with a post show a label made of a symbol and a word: Reserved (entry held for that day), Drafting (being written), Drafted (ready to review). A day can hold more than one post. Click a day to see its details and the next action. c1 c2

Task 4: review a post, then publish it by hand 1e

  1. Open a post from Posts. Read it. Quality-check notes sit beside it. They are advice only and never approve anything. d1
  2. Choose Approve, Request edits or Reject. An approval applies to that exact version. d1
  3. If you edit the text, saving creates a new version and the old approval no longer counts. The screen says so before you save. d2
  4. On a phone the same screen is one column, with the three buttons fixed at the bottom. d4
  5. After approval, press Publish on LinkedIn. Copy the text, post it on LinkedIn yourself, then paste back the post link and the date. Virel never posts for you. d3 d5

Task 5: research, then a post from a finding 1f

  1. Open Research. Edit your saved prompt, then press Run now or pick a weekly schedule. e1
  2. Open a briefing. Each finding lists its sources and dates. e2
  3. Press Save to Content Bank. The finding becomes a trend reaction entry. e3
  4. From that entry, press Create post or Add to Calendar, exactly as in Tasks 1 and 2. Research never creates a post or an approval by itself.

Task 6: write and approve a newsletter edition 1i

  1. Open Newsletter. You see your editions and their status. Press New edition. f1
  2. Choose the sources: Content Bank entries, research briefings, saved documents. Press Draft edition. f2
  3. Read the draft. Quality-check notes sit beside it and are advice only. Approve the exact version, request edits, or edit it. f3
  4. Once approved, copy the text or export it. f4

Empty, loading, error and no-permission screens 1g

Every page shows its title and menu at once, with gray placeholder bars where data is still loading. Empty pages say what to do first. Errors say what failed and that nothing was changed. If you lack a permission, the button is dimmed with one line saying who can grant it. If a whole area is off-limits to you, it is hidden. The small screens in 1g show each case.
