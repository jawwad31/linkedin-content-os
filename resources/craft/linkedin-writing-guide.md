# LinkedIn Writing Guide

Write direct, specific, operational B2B LinkedIn prose. The post should sound
like a knowledgeable founder speaking to one relevant buyer, not an AI
summarizing a planning document.

## Writing Sequence

Draft in this order inside one AI call:

1. Validate the plan against source, Approved Evidence, and claim boundaries.
2. Write the body from the argument outline and selected framework.
3. Write the hook that accurately promises what the finished body delivers.
4. Write the selected CTA or intentionally leave it empty.
5. Read the assembled post once for continuity, proof, voice, and public-copy
   hygiene.

This order prevents a dramatic hook from forcing the body into unsupported
claims.

## Proof-Safe Voice

Every factual claim must trace to at least one of:

- the selected Founder Experience;
- the selected Trend Reaction;
- the selected TrendCard and its cited articles;
- Approved Evidence;
- saved business facts such as the offer or industry.

When evidence is absent:

- be qualitatively precise;
- state an argument as a bounded POV;
- remove the number, name, quote, or causal claim;
- never fill a gap with a plausible detail.

Examples:

- Prefer "the handoff was the constraint" to an invented percentage.
- Prefer "start by checking the segment" to "the segment is always the
  problem."
- Prefer "this shift may change evaluation criteria" to a guaranteed forecast.

## Source-Mode Voice

### Founder Experience

First person is allowed for documented events, actions, lessons, and outcomes.
Preserve the source's actual scope. One founder event does not prove a universal
market pattern.

Allowed shapes:

- "We changed..."
- "I learned..."
- "The decision that mattered was..."

Only use "we saw," "I noticed," or dialogue when the source contains the
corresponding observation or words.

### TrendCard

Write as market analysis. Attribute important claims to the signal or sources
when useful. Do not use founder-lived-experience framing.

Allowed shapes:

- "The shift suggests..."
- "For [ICP], the implication is..."
- "The evidence points to..."

Avoid:

- "We've seen this with clients";
- "In my experience";
- "We tested this";
- any founder result not supplied separately as Approved Evidence.

### Trend Reaction

Use the founder's saved reaction as the POV and the TrendCard as timing/context.
First person is allowed only to express the supplied view or a documented
example in the reaction.

Do not intensify "this might matter" into "this will transform the market."

## Founder Voice and Inspiration Posts

Use the voice sample, phrases to use, phrases to avoid, and up to three
inspiration posts only for style calibration.

You may learn:

- sentence length and cadence;
- directness;
- paragraph density;
- use of fragments or parenthetical asides;
- level of warmth, challenge, or restraint;
- how the founder transitions between points.

You may not copy:

- claims, stories, metrics, results, names, quotes, or examples;
- distinctive sentences or multi-word phrases;
- the inspiration post's topic when unrelated to the approved idea;
- its CTA or resource offer unless independently selected.

The target is the same writing character, not imitation.

## Tone

Use:

- simple words;
- short paragraphs;
- varied sentence rhythm;
- specific operational language;
- calm conviction;
- useful nuance;
- contractions when natural.

Avoid:

- corporate jargon;
- generic thought-leadership language;
- hype and exaggerated certainty;
- fake vulnerability;
- a preachy or superior tone;
- excessive rhetorical questions;
- talking about "content," "the audience," or "engagement" instead of the
  buyer's problem.

## Anti-Slop Rules

Never use:

- "game-changing," "revolutionary," "unlock," "secret," or "crushing it"
  unless the client explicitly requires the phrase;
- "in today's fast-paced world";
- "here's the thing" as a default transition;
- "let that sink in";
- "read that again";
- "agree?";
- "thoughts?";
- "follow for more";
- generic "most founders/teams/companies" claims without source support;
- fabricated "people ask me all the time" framing;
- emoji-numbered section headers;
- internal strategy, taxonomy, QA, source ID, or workflow labels.

Do not publish labels such as:

- Current belief
- Desired belief
- Core argument
- Buyer pain
- Commercial relevance
- Proof notes
- Risk notes
- GenerationPlanV2
- Hook / Body / CTA

## Sentence Rhythm

Use a mix:

```
Short claim.

A slightly longer explanation that adds a real detail or distinction.

Then the consequence.
```

Fragments are useful for emphasis. Use them sparingly.

Keep most paragraphs to one or two sentences. Do not put every sentence on its
own line when natural grouping improves comprehension.

## Lists and Section Labels

Use lists when the selected structure or framework benefits from parallel
items. Each item should add a distinct part of the argument.

Use:

- plain numbered lists when order matters;
- dashes when order does not matter;
- arrows for a short process or transition;
- plain-text labels when they genuinely improve navigation.

Do not create several shallow sections merely to make the post look long.

## Length

There is no required character target. Use these only as broad editing ranges:

- focused insight or POV: approximately 900-1,700 characters;
- story, framework, or list: approximately 1,400-2,400 characters;
- detailed breakdown: approximately 2,000-2,900 characters.

Shorter and complete is better than longer and padded. A post may fall outside
these ranges when the source and argument justify it.

## Commercial Relevance

Teach first. Make the connection to the offer through the buyer's problem,
decision, or desired outcome.

Avoid:

- inserting the offer into every post;
- claiming the service solves an unsupported problem;
- turning the final paragraph into a sales pitch;
- adding urgency not present in the source.

An open-door CTA may connect to the offer when genuine. A resource post may
offer only its selected approved resource.

## Resource-Post Copy

A resource post must remain valuable without receiving the asset. Explain:

- the problem the resource helps solve;
- one or more useful checks, distinctions, or steps;
- who the resource is for;
- what it contains, using only the saved resource description;
- the approved keyword once in the CTA.

Do not claim automatic delivery. Do not invent pages, templates, sections,
results, or contents not present in the Content Resource.

## Draft Completion Check

Before returning the structured draft, verify:

- the body follows the stored core argument and outline;
- the hook is supported and delivered by the body;
- every number, quote, name, result, and event is traceable;
- first-person framing matches the source mode;
- the CTA is empty for `none` and singular for every other type;
- comment gating has an approved selected resource;
- banned topics and phrases to avoid are absent;
- inspiration posts influenced style only;
- internal scaffolding is absent;
- the post ends when the argument is complete.
