# LinkedIn Body Frameworks

<!-- playbook-section:universal -->
Use exactly one primary body framework. A framework is the reasoning sequence
inside the selected post structure. Never announce its name in the public copy.

Universal body rules:

- follow the stored argument outline unless proof safety requires narrowing it;
- keep one core argument and one narrative thread;
- place evidence next to the claim it supports;
- use short paragraphs and simple language;
- turn the source into a useful reader implication;
- use qualitative precision when no number is approved;
- do not add a story, case, quote, metric, tool, or example to make the post
  feel richer;
- stop when the argument is complete.
<!-- /playbook-section -->

<!-- playbook-section:selection -->
Select one framework:

- `aida`: attention, interest, desire, action;
- `pas`: problem, real cost, solution;
- `bab`: documented before, documented after, bridge;
- `mistake_lesson`: sourced mistake, consequence, lesson, operating rule;
- `contrarian_reframe`: common view, limitation, better frame, nuance;
- `one_thing`: context, one insight, support, reader application.

Compatibility:

- BAB needs real before and after states.
- Mistake to Lesson needs a Founder Experience or an explicit founder mistake
  in a Trend Reaction.
- Contrarian Reframe needs a specific belief worth disagreeing with.
- AIDA is useful for teaching or a carefully framed resource post, but "desire"
  means making a better operating state concrete, not hype.
- One Thing is preferred when the source is narrow or evidence is limited.
<!-- /playbook-section -->

<!-- playbook-section:aida -->
## AIDA

Use for educational posts, resource posts, and documented case material.

Sequence:

1. Attention: use the planned hook to state the claim or tension.
2. Interest: explain the target buyer's specific situation and why the obvious
   approach is incomplete.
3. Desire: make the better state, method, or decision concrete.
4. Action: close with one planned next step, or no CTA.

Writing prompts:

- What does the buyer recognize immediately?
- What detail deepens the tension without padding?
- What would improve if the buyer adopted the desired belief?
- What is the smallest credible next step?

Do not turn Desire into promotional adjectives or unsupported results. For a
resource post, teach the key principle before offering the resource.
<!-- /playbook-section -->

<!-- playbook-section:pas -->
## PAS

Use for pain-point posts and diagnosis-driven arguments.

Sequence:

1. Problem: name the specific issue the target buyer experiences.
2. Agitate: show the real operating cost, risk, delay, or confusion.
3. Solution: present the source-supported frame, check, or approach.

Writing prompts:

- What symptom does the buyer already see?
- What happens if they keep addressing the wrong layer?
- What should they inspect, believe, or do instead?

Agitation must clarify stakes, not create fear. Do not invent monetary losses,
failure rates, or urgency. The solution should be useful without becoming a
pitch for the offer.
<!-- /playbook-section -->

<!-- playbook-section:bab -->
## BAB

Use for real transformations and documented operating changes.

Sequence:

1. Before: describe the actual starting state.
2. After: describe the actual improved or changed state.
3. Bridge: explain the documented changes connecting them.

Writing prompts:

- Which source details establish the before state?
- Which details establish the after state?
- Which actions are documented, and which causal links remain uncertain?
- What transferable lesson survives without overclaiming?

Keep timeframes, metrics, and named outcomes exact. If the source establishes
correlation but not causality, say "after we changed" rather than "because we
changed." If either state is absent, do not use BAB.
<!-- /playbook-section -->

<!-- playbook-section:mistake_lesson -->
## Mistake to Lesson

Use only for a documented founder/operator mistake.

Sequence:

1. State the real decision or assumption.
2. Show what happened.
3. Explain the consequence using only documented detail.
4. Name the lesson.
5. Convert it into an operating rule the buyer can use.

Writing prompts:

- What exactly did the founder decide or miss?
- What consequence is in the record?
- What changed in their thinking?
- What can the reader check without copying the founder's situation?

Do not invent embarrassment, conflict, dialogue, or dramatic stakes. The lesson
should not imply that one event proves a universal rule.
<!-- /playbook-section -->

<!-- playbook-section:contrarian_reframe -->
## Contrarian Reframe

Use for a defensible disagreement that improves a buyer decision.

Sequence:

1. State the common or current view accurately.
2. Explain where it fails for this ICP or situation.
3. Present the better frame and its reasoning.
4. Add nuance: identify when the old view may still apply.
5. End with the practical consequence.

Writing prompts:

- What exactly is being challenged?
- What source detail demonstrates the limitation?
- What new frame is more useful?
- What boundary keeps the claim credible?

Do not build a straw man. No "everyone says" language without evidence. A
contrarian post should be precise, not louder.
<!-- /playbook-section -->

<!-- playbook-section:one_thing -->
## One Thing

Use for narrow sources, focused observations, and high-signal short posts.

Sequence:

1. Give one line of context.
2. State one insight.
3. Support it with the strongest available source detail.
4. Explain what the target buyer can check, decide, or do.
5. Close.

Writing prompts:

- If only one sentence could survive, which is it?
- What source detail makes that sentence credible?
- What changes for the reader?

Do not inflate one insight into a list. This framework should feel deliberate,
not incomplete.
<!-- /playbook-section -->
