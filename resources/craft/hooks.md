# LinkedIn Hooks

<!-- playbook-section:universal -->
The hook is the first public promise of the post. It must express the core
claim, tension, diagnosis, or sourced result quickly enough that the target
buyer recognizes why the post matters.

Hook rules:

1. Keep it under roughly 210 characters when possible.
2. Make it derivable from the selected source and GenerationPlanV2.
3. Use a number, name, quote, or result only when supplied by the selected
   source or Approved Evidence.
4. Use first-person observation only when the source mode permits it.
5. Open with the claim or tension, not background or an announcement.
6. Write one final hook for production, not a menu of alternatives.
7. Make sure the body can fully deliver the promise.

Avoid:

- "I'm excited to announce";
- yes/no questions;
- generic "most founders/teams" claims without evidence;
- curiosity gaps, "secrets," or manufactured controversy;
- hashtags, emoji headers, and engagement instructions;
- a resource keyword in the hook;
- repeating the planned angle as an internal label.
<!-- /playbook-section -->

<!-- playbook-section:selection -->
Select one hook type:

- `declarative_pov`: direct defensible thesis;
- `diagnostic`: visible symptom contrasted with root cause;
- `belief_crack`: old belief followed by the better belief;
- `specific_result`: sourced action and outcome;
- `before_after`: documented weak and strong states;
- `direct_address`: precise target-buyer situation;
- `stakes`: concrete consequence of delay or a wrong decision.

Compatibility:

- `specific_result` requires an exact result in Founder Experience or Approved
  Evidence.
- `before_after` requires both states.
- `diagnostic` requires a source-supported root cause, not speculation.
- TrendCard-only posts should usually use declarative POV, diagnostic, belief
  crack, direct address, or stakes.
- Resource posts still open with buyer value. Do not open with "Comment X."
<!-- /playbook-section -->

<!-- playbook-section:declarative_pov -->
## Declarative POV

State the core argument flatly and, when useful, add the practical consequence.

Shapes:

- `[Defensible claim]. [Why it matters].`
- `[Common operating choice] solves the wrong layer. [Better frame].`
- `[Direct belief]. Here is the practical consequence.`

Use when the value is clarity and conviction. The claim should be something the
founder would defend on a sales call.

Do not:

- add "unpopular opinion" to manufacture distinction;
- use broad social proof such as "everyone is doing this";
- overstate a TrendCard as certainty.
<!-- /playbook-section -->

<!-- playbook-section:diagnostic -->
## Diagnostic

Start with an observable symptom and identify the likely layer to inspect.

Shapes:

- `If [symptom], the problem may not be [obvious culprit]. Check [root layer].`
- `[Visible metric] looks healthy. [Hidden constraint] is still broken.`
- `You can spot [problem] when [observable signal].`

Use when the source supports the relationship between symptom and cause.

Qualify with "often," "may," or "start by checking" when causality is not
proven. Do not turn one case into a universal diagnosis.
<!-- /playbook-section -->

<!-- playbook-section:belief_crack -->
## Belief Crack

Put the current and desired beliefs in productive tension.

Shapes:

- `[Current belief]. [More accurate belief].`
- `The goal is not [old priority]. It is [new priority].`
- `[Common fix] feels productive. It leaves [actual constraint] untouched.`

Use when the belief shift is the post's strongest reason to continue reading.

Do not create a false binary. Preserve nuance the argument needs.
<!-- /playbook-section -->

<!-- playbook-section:specific_result -->
## Specific Result

Open with a documented action and result.

Shapes:

- `We [documented action]. [Documented outcome].`
- `[Approved result] came from [documented change], not [previous assumption].`
- `In [approved timeframe], [approved before/after contrast].`

Required:

- exact support for every metric, timeframe, name, and outcome;
- source mode that permits first person or Approved Evidence that identifies
  whose result it is;
- body content that explains the result without implying unsupported causality.

If any required detail is missing, use declarative POV or diagnostic instead.
<!-- /playbook-section -->

<!-- playbook-section:before_after -->
## Before / After

Contrast two documented states and point toward the bridge.

Shapes:

- `Before: [real weak state]. After: [real strong state]. The difference was [real change].`
- `We moved from [documented before] to [documented after] by changing [bridge].`
- `[Old state] looked like progress. [New state] changed the decision.`

Use only when both states are present. Do not imply numerical improvement,
speed, or causality not established by the evidence.
<!-- /playbook-section -->

<!-- playbook-section:direct_address -->
## Direct Address

Name a precise buyer situation so the intended reader self-identifies.

Shapes:

- `If you are [role] facing [specific decision], check [specific issue] first.`
- `For [specific ICP stage], [common approach] creates [specific risk].`
- `When [buyer situation], the first question is not [obvious question].`

Use the full situation, not just the job title. Avoid "this is for you" or
"stop scrolling."
<!-- /playbook-section -->

<!-- playbook-section:stakes -->
## Stakes

Lead with the real cost of delay, misdiagnosis, or a poor choice.

Shapes:

- `The expensive part is not [fix]. It is [documented or qualitative cost of delay].`
- `Every cycle spent on [wrong layer] leaves [actual constraint] unchanged.`
- `[Decision] gets harder when [compounding consequence].`

Use qualitative stakes when no number is approved. Do not invent urgency,
deadlines, losses, or fear.
<!-- /playbook-section -->
