# Hooks

The hook is the first visible part of the LinkedIn post. Keep the first 210 characters sharp, specific, and relevant to the target buyer.

## Good Hooks

- Start with the buyer's real tension.
- Use specific language from the buyer's world.
- Create useful tension without clickbait.
- Point toward the belief shift.
- Avoid context-setting before the reader has a reason to care.

## Useful Patterns

- Buyer pain: "[Buyer] does not have a [surface problem]. They have a [deeper problem]."
- Market shift: "[Old playbook] worked when [old condition]. It breaks when [new condition]."
- Operator lesson: "The mistake I see in [situation]: [specific mistake]."
- Contrarian but useful: "The problem is not [common diagnosis]. It is [sharper diagnosis]."
- Before/after: "Before [change], [old behavior]. Now, [new buyer reality]."

## Avoid

- "I am excited to announce..."
- Empty questions.
- Unsupported numbers.
- Generic "hot takes."
- Hooks written for creators instead of buyers.
- Claims the post cannot deliver.
