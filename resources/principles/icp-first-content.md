# ICP-First Content

ICP-first content makes the target buyer feel understood. It should sound like it was written from inside the buyer's operating reality, not from generic LinkedIn advice.

## Quality Standard

Strong content should:

- Name a real buyer pain or tension.
- Connect the pain to a business consequence.
- Reflect the founder's operator perspective.
- Move the buyer from an old belief to a sharper new belief.
- Stay commercially relevant to the offer without becoming a pitch.
- Be specific enough that the ICP recognizes themselves.

## Selected-Reader Relevance

Every post specifies an intended reader (e.g. buyers, practitioners, peers, or specialists). When an intended reader is specified, calibrate the post's tone, tension, and vocabulary to that reader rather than defaulting blindly to generic buyer ICP assumptions. Content for practitioners should respect practitioner craft; content for peers should respect peer standards.

## Avoid

- Writing for generic creators or clickbait audiences.
- Broad applause lines that any audience could agree with.
- Motivation without operating insight.
- Trend commentary without reader relevance.
- Content that attracts engagement from the wrong audience.
- Claims that sound impressive but do not help the reader think better.

## Buyer Belief Shift

Every final post should create a shift:

```text
From: [what the buyer currently believes]
To: [what the post helps them believe instead]
```

Examples of belief shift patterns:

- From "this is a volume problem" to "this is a relevance problem."
- From "we need more content" to "we need sharper buyer insight."
- From "the market changed" to "our operating assumptions need to change."

Do not force a dramatic contrarian take. The shift can be subtle if it is useful.
