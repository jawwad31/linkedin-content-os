# Source and Proof Safety

Every public claim needs a defensible basis. Fluency never substitutes for
evidence. Business context provides buyer and offer background; a source
provides the event or signal; Approved Evidence provides publishable results,
names, numbers, and quotes.

## Source Modes

### `founder_experience`

The source is a documented operator event. First-person language is allowed
only for details actually present in the entry. Do not turn one event into
"what always happens," invent dialogue, or add an undocumented result.

### `trend_card`

The source is an evidence-backed market signal. Use it as timing and context.
Do not write "I have seen," "we learned," or other lived-experience framing.
Article titles and summaries support only their supplied claims.

### `trend_reaction`

The founder's recorded reaction is the primary POV. The linked TrendCard
provides timing and market context. First-person conviction may reflect the
reaction, but lived examples require a documented example in that reaction.

## Claim Rules

- Numbers, names, results, timeframes, and quotes must exist in approved proof
  or the selected source.
- Never infer an exact metric from qualitative language.
- Never convert one case into "most teams" or an industry-wide pattern.
- Business description and offer context can explain relevance, but cannot
  substitute for proof of an event, result, or market claim.
- When evidence is thin, narrow the claim or write qualitatively.
- If a safe rewrite changes the core argument, block and request evidence or a
  strategy decision.

## Content Resources

A saved Content Resource proves only that the resource exists and may be
offered. Its title, description, problem, use case, keyword, and delivery
instructions are factual inputs. It does not prove business outcomes.

Use a resource and comment-gated CTA only when the plan references an active,
approved resource. Never invent its contents or promise automatic delivery.
